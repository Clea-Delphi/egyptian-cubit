The Egyptian Cubit Ruler

The royal cubit (meh niswt) divided into 28 fingers, each named for a god of the solar barque — with the phi mark, the equatorial foot, and the harmonic cascade within each finger.

The 28 gods are from E.A. Wallis Budge, The Gods of the Egyptians, Vol. 2, pp. 291–292. Budge gives each name and hieroglyph; the meanings are his where noted, and marked uncertain where they are not.

The Harmonic Cascade — 15 Sub-Units in One Finger

Each finger (0.7406 in) divides into 15 nested units, each n/(n+1): 1/2, 2/3, 3/4 … 15/16. They converge toward the whole finger.

The Eye of Horus Fractions

1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + 1/16 + 1/32 + 1/64 = 63/64. The missing 1/64 is the "Thoth magic" that restores wholeness.

Honest note: modern scholarship (Jim Ritter) shows these fractions originated as hekat capacity submultiples in hieratic administrative grain records, long before the Eye of Horus myth. The mythological framing is a 20th-century popularization riding on the visual similarity to the wedjat glyph.

The 1/7 Hieroglyph Column

Reported practice, source not yet located: the column that hieroglyphs were made on is 1/7 the width of a cubit, 4 fingers. The 7-fold division of the cubit is documented (7 palms, 28 fingers); the column-width reading is a lead, not a citation.

The Old Kingdom Canon — the 6×6 Division of the Cubit

The Egyptian drawing canon set the standing figure at 18 squares from soles to hairline. The canonical figure height is 3 royal cubits, so each grid square is exactly 1/6 of a royal cubit. The cubit divides into six parts, and a 6×6 grid of the cubit makes 36 cells.

Honest note: the "6 feet" echo (18 fists, 3 fists per foot) depends on which foot is meant; the clean, verifiable relationship is the 1/6-cubit grid module.
